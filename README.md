# swift-acoustics-fracture

Use this package when a sound comes from something brittle breaking, such as a window shattering: a crack runs out from the break at the material's terminal speed and frees fragments whose sizes follow from the material's toughness and how fast it was strained; each fragment rings, as a small free plate, from the bend it was cut with, falls, tumbles and bounces on the floor, and every landing ticks and rings it again through Hertz's contact. Start from `Acoustics.Fracture.Pane`, break it with `shatter(x:y:rate:floor:air:seed:)`, and read the sound pressure a distance away from the `Acoustics.Fracture.Shatter` with `pressures(count:interval:distance:)`; the room and the floor's reflection are the room's work, not this package's.

## Laws

- `Material.glass`, soda-lime window glass, is E = 72 GPa, ρ = 2500 kg/m³, ν = 0.22 (Shelby 2005), γ = 3.9 J/m² (Wiederhorn 1969), a terminal crack speed of 1500 m/s (Schardin 1959), a loss factor of 0.001 (inside Cremer, Heckl and Petersson's 0.0006 to 0.002 for glass) and a modulus of rupture of 41 MPa, annealed float glass's mean breaking stress (GANA FM05-12, 2023; Pilkington ATS-129).
- Its toughness is Griffith's (1921) energy balance in Irwin's form, K_IC = √(2 γ E / (1 − ν²)): 0.768 MPa√m, within 5% of the measured 0.75. Griffith's strength is σ = √(2 E γ / (π a)).
- The mean fragment size is Grady's (1982), s = (√20 K_IC / (ρ c ε̇))^(2/3) with c = √(E / ρ); fragment areas follow Grady and Kipp's (1985) linear exponential with mean s² until they fill the pane, and the last is cut so they sum to its area exactly. A fragment is freed when the crack front reaches it, its distance from the break over the crack speed.
- A fragment rings in the three lowest bending modes of a free square of its area, from swift-acoustics-plate's finite elements on a 16 × 16 grid, within 1% of Leissa's (1969) λ² = 13.4728, 19.5961 and 24.2702 at ν = 0.3; between the squares solved, √2 apart in side, f a² and the shapes are interpolated in log side. Shapes are scaled to a mean square of one, so a mode at speed V carries ½ m V².
- When it breaks, the pane is bent as Timoshenko and Woinowsky-Krieger's (1959, §19) simply supported circular plate of its area loaded at the break, M_r = P (1 + ν) ln(R / r) / 4π and M_t = M_r + P (1 − ν) / 4π, with P putting the hoop stress 6 M_t / h² at the load's equivalent radius 0.325 h to the modulus of rupture. A fragment is cut free with the curvatures there, κ_r = 12 (M_r − ν M_t) / (E h³) and across, and each mode starts displaced by that bend's share in it, at rest: a released bend gives the modes no more than its strain energy, ½ D κ² per area.
- It falls freely under g = 9.806 65 m/s² (3rd CGPM, 1901), seen edge on as a rod of its side with I = m a² / 12, its tilt drawn evenly from the seed since nothing here says how it leaves the frame. A landing is a planar rigid-body impact at the lower end (Brach 1989; Stronge 2000): the floor feels m / (1 + m r² / I), a quarter of the mass for a corner of a flat fragment. Bounces end below 5 cm/s, after 32 landings or after 4 s.
- `Floor.douglasFir` is flat-sawn Douglas-fir across the grain, E_R and μ_LR from swift-acoustics-plate's Wood Handbook (2021) Douglas-fir, crushing at 5.5 MPa perpendicular to the grain (Table 5–3a). A landing's restitution is Johnson's (1985, §11.5) fully plastic impact of a sphere of half the thickness, e = 3.8 (Y / E*)^½ (½ m v² / (Y R³))^(−1/8), no more than 1: about 0.3 for a 4 mm glass corner at 4 m/s, rising as v^(−1/4) as the bounces slow. No measured friction of glass on wood was to hand, so corners land without it.
- A landing's contact is the impact chain: the floor, through `Contact.hertz` with the corner a sphere of half the thickness, strikes a swift-acoustics-resonator holding the fragment's motion at the corner (no frequency, the felt mass) and its bending modes (reach φ cos tilt, φ the shape at the corner), stepped at 1/400 of Hertz's 2.94 δ* / v. A rigid corner presses for Hertz's time to 1% and takes (1 + e) m v to 0.5%; a slow mode rings at φ J / m to 2%. Hertz is elastic, so the force and the rings are scaled by (1 + e) / 2.
- A mode decays at σ = π f (η + η_rad), with the radiation loss 2 ρ₀ c σ_rad / (ω ρ h) of both faces (Cremer, Heckl and Petersson 2005), and is heard `distance` r away at amplitude ρ₀ c V √(σ_rad S / 2π) / r: each face radiating as swift-acoustics-plate's baffled Rayleigh integral gives, spread over a sphere. A baffle is right above coincidence, where most shards ring, and too generous below it, where a free edge short-circuits. A mode at or above half the sampling rate is left out.
- A landing's tick is the jolt's dipole, p = (cos ϑ / 4π c r) d/dt [(m_a + ρ₀ V) a] (Koss and Alfredson 1973), with the face's entrained air m_a = 8 ρ₀ b³ / 3 of the disk of its area (Lamb 1932) along its normal, at its mean over directions, cos ϑ → 1/√3; each sample is the moment's change across it, so a tick sums to nothing and small fragments, whose modes are above hearing, still tick.
- Surface energy and toughness are plain numbers (J/m² and Pa√m) until they have quantity atoms of their own.
